Magnetic fields play an important role in many areas of science and technology – from particle accelerators and MRI machines to measurement systems that allow us to look deeper into physical phenomena.
The strongest continuous magnetic fields achieved in a laboratory approach 45 Tesla, while pulsed-field magnets can reach as high as 100 Tesla for very short periods of time. To put this into perspective, a fridge magnet produces a field of 1-10 millitesla.
But how can we achieve such high fields? In this talk, I will introduce the technology behind high-field magnets and how they are designed and built. I will focus on Bitter resistive magnets and superconducting magnets that are used in laboratories around the world (including Albanova).

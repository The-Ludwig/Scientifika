Electrons are elementary particles and therefore indivisible. Common sense tells us that charges in electron systems should come in whole multiples of the elementary charge, e. And yet, a collection of electrons confined to a 2D sheet may conspire to split charge into e/3, e/5, and other fractions. This is the fractional quantum Hall effect, where a strong magnetic field and Coulomb interactions produce an exotic many-body state. In this talk I will explain how this phenomenon works. I will then show that the magnetic field is not essential: in some materials, the topology of the electronic bands, or even the vacuum fluctuations of light, can play its role. Finally, I will give a glimpse into how non-Abelian states—potential building blocks for fault-tolerant quantum computers—may emerge in these systems.
